Mission

To create player-driven experiences worthy of a place within the narrative of the King, to challenge harmful practices, and to edify and unify those who play their games.

Values

Worthy

Scope creep is a major challenge in game development, and we want to guard against an excessive amount of it. However, completed work should consist of a well-rounded package, with no missing pieces within a well-defined and comprehensive scope (Luke 14: 28-32). In addition, work is finished when it displays a level of care, attention to detail, and polish that we are proud of presenting not to humans, but to the King (1 Corinthians 10:31).

Within the Narrative of the King

The King created each of us with a unique role and purpose in His plan (Ephesians 2:10). We are created in His image, with His creative nature, and He gave us His creation to steward and to work (Genesis 1:26-28). In addition, good and evil exist in opposition to each other as absolute truths as defined by the King (Genesis 3). Choosing one over the other has consequences that affect this earth and eternity, and the greatest choice one can make is to accept the King's gift of forgiveness offered through His Son (Romans 3:23, 1 John 1:9). As those called to be a light in a dark world, we seek to ensure that our work and actions reflect the King's love, purity, and goodness as revealed in His Word and in living in personal relationship with Him (Matthew 5:14-16).

Player-Driven

Our games are not intended to convey specific messages made by humans, but rather to allow everyone who engages with them to be spoken to on a personal level (1 Thessalonians 2:13, 2 Peter 1:20-21). Games possess the unique characteristic of allowing a player to explore for themselves all facets of a story or issue and decide which outcomes seem best. Our games should allow players to choose their own path and decide for themselves whether they like the consequences of their decisions, with the hope that the King may guide them to reflection that leads to edification (Colossians 3:2).

Challenge Harmful Practices

Some behaviors in the gaming industry do not align with what the King says is good, from manipulative tactics intended to take advantage of players for monetary gain, to games that promote gratuitous immorality. Our aim is to take a stand against and call out these practices and to provide audiences with experiences that are beneficial instead of detrimental to them (Ephesians 6:12).

Edify and Unify

As bearers of the image of our loving and relational King, we are created and called to love and live in relationship (Galatians 3:28, Romans 8:17, John 1:12). We do not work in isolation but strive for peace and mutual upbuilding among those who serve the King (Romans 14:19, 1 Corinthians 1:10, Ephesians 4:2-6).
